Projector or 85-inch panel? Planning a home theater

Very large flat panels have changed the math on dedicated theaters. How we decide between a projector and a panel, and where the sound comes in.

Written by

Cooper Technology Group

Need help with this?

(877) 266-7379

Most homes have one main place to watch: a family room, a living room or a den. Some have a dedicated theater. Both deserve a system designed for the room rather than bought off a shelf.

The dedicated theater

A traditional theater has a large screen, a high definition projector on the ceiling, a surround receiver and amplifier, and a set of speakers that can be visible or hidden. The equipment is often located elsewhere, in a closet or a rack room, so it does not take space or attention.

Projectors still deliver the largest image. They also need upkeep, including periodic lamp replacement on many models, and they want a room where light can be controlled.

The case for a panel

As prices on 80 to 90 inch panels have come down, many of our clients now choose one instead of a projector and screen. Total cost of ownership is usually lower, there is less maintenance, and a panel copes far better with daylight. In many rooms it is simply the better answer.

Pick the display for the room, not the brochure.

Sound for everyday rooms

Family rooms increasingly use a soundbar mounted above or below the TV. A Sonos soundbar sounds excellent on its own, and can add wireless rear speakers and a subwoofer later. It also streams music, so the TV area becomes a music room too.

One button, years later

With Control4, the whole room starts with one press: lights dim, Lutron shades close, the screen and sound come on. When you call us with a question years later, you speak to someone who knows how your system was put in and which parts are worth upgrading.
